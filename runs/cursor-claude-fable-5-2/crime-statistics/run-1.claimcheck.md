# ClaimCheck — "Crime has never been this bad"

**Source:** `post.md`, read 2026-11-11. Unchanged.
**Central claim:** > "It is the worst it has ever been in this country, and anyone who says otherwise is not paying attention."
**Summary:** clear failures: 3 · partial: 3 · passed: 0 · N/A: 1
**Score:** 1.5 / 6 (1 N/A)

The problem sits in magnitude, boundary, and evidence: a national
count with no rate and no earlier year beside it, one city and one
feed carried to the whole country and to all of history, and no
named source for any figure. Mechanism, recency, and the remedy are
partial; output does not apply. A legitimate concern survives: a
rise in one city's murders may be real, about half of violent crime
and most property crime never reaches police, and the national
motor vehicle theft rate did climb through 2023. This is a literacy
review. It does not rule the claim true or false.

## Claims read

- **C-1** (central) > "Crime is out of control. It is the worst it has ever been in this country, and anyone who says otherwise is not paying attention."
- **C-2** > "More than a million violent crimes were reported last year. A million."
- **C-3** > "In my city, murders went from 30 to 40 in a single year. That is a 33 percent jump"
- **C-4** > "what is happening here is happening everywhere."
- **C-5** > "My neighborhood app shows a new break-in or car theft every single day. A few years ago I never saw any of that."
- **C-6** > "the official numbers can't be trusted anyway, because most crime never gets reported."
- **C-7** (side claim) > "The only answer is more police on every street, whatever it costs."

## Findings

### 1 · Mechanistic literacy — partial

> "More than a million violent crimes were reported last year." · "the official numbers can't be trusted anyway, because most crime never gets reported." · "My neighborhood app shows a new break-in or car theft every single day. A few years ago I never saw any of that."

Claims: C-2, C-5, C-6
Why: Half right. C-2 says "reported," which is what a police
figure counts, and C-6 is right that a large share of crime never
reaches police. But C-6 throws the measures out instead of naming
the survey built to count unreported crime, and C-5 reads what a
feed shows as what occurred, when a feed counts posts, and "never
saw" depends on who was posting and whether the reader was on the
app.
Contrast: BJS, *Criminal Victimization, 2024* (September 2025),
Table 4: 47.9% of violent victimizations and 30.5% of property
victimizations were reported to police in 2024, so 100 − 47.9 =
52.1% and 100 − 30.5 = 69.5% were not. The same survey counts both
kinds, and its appendix table 1 shows the total and the reported
part moving together: 79.8 and 33.8 per 1,000 persons age 12 or
older in 1993, 23.3 and 11.2 in 2024. Under-reporting sets the
level of a police count; it does not by itself say which way crime
is moving. The FBI's 2025 summary ("Differences in Crime Measures")
describes the survey as collecting crimes "whether or not those
crimes were reported to law enforcement."
<https://bjs.ojp.gov/document/cv24.pdf> ·
<https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/LATEST/resources/reports/UCR_Summary_of_Reported_Crimes_in_the_Nation_2025.pdf>
Next step: BJS, *Criminal Victimization, 2024*, Table 4 (percent
reported to police, by type of crime) and appendix table 1 (total
and reported rates, 1993–2024). For C-5, the city police
department's incident data for burglary and motor vehicle theft in
that neighborhood, over the same years as the app posts.

### 2 · Quantitative and comparative literacy — clear failure

> "More than a million violent crimes were reported last year. A million." · "It is the worst it has ever been in this country"

Claims: C-1, C-2, C-3
Why: An absolute count with no denominator and no earlier year
beside it, offered in support of a claim about all of history.
"Worst ever" is a comparison, and the post never supplies the other
side of it. C-3 does give its baseline, and the arithmetic holds
((40 − 30) / 30 = 33.3%), but it has no population or rate and
compares only two years of a small count.
Contrast: FBI, *UCR Summary of Reported Crimes in the Nation, 2025*
(released August 2026): an estimated 1,119,768 violent crimes in
2025, a rate of 327.6 per 100,000 inhabitants, or about 1 per 305
inhabitants (100,000 / 327.6 = 305). Three comparisons:

- 1991: 1,911,767 violent crimes, 758.2 per 100,000 (FBI, *Crime in
  the United States, 2010*, Table 1; 1991 is the earliest year in
  that table). 327.6 / 758.2 = 0.43, so the 2025 rate is 57% lower.
  The count is lower too: 1,119,768 / 1,911,767 = 0.59.
- 2006: the four offense rates in the 2025 summary's Figure 1 sum
  to 5.7 + 29.8 + 150.1 + 291.7 = 477.3 per 100,000. 327.6 / 477.3
  = 0.69, so the 2025 rate is 31% lower. The FBI broadened its rape
  definition in 2013, so the 2006 sum is understated against
  today's definition.
- 2024: 362.9 per 100,000 (5.1 + 39.2 + 61.0 + 257.6), and 2025 is
  4.1 + 36.1 + 49.4 + 238.0 = 327.6. The same sum for every other
  year in Figure 1 (2006–2023) falls between 368.6 and 477.3, so
  2025 is the lowest of the 20 years shown.

A count above a million is therefore consistent with the lowest
rate in that window.
<https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/LATEST/resources/reports/UCR_Summary_of_Reported_Crimes_in_the_Nation_2025.pdf> ·
<https://ucr.fbi.gov/crime-in-the-u.s/2010/crime-in-the-u.s.-2010/tables/10tbl01.xls>
Next step: FBI Crime Data Explorer, *Crime in the United States,
2025*, Table 1 (volume and rate per 100,000 inhabitants by year).
Read the rate column across years, not the count. For C-3, the
city's murders per 100,000 residents over ten or more years.

### 3 · Scale and boundary literacy — clear failure

> "In my city, murders went from 30 to 40 in a single year. That is a 33 percent jump, and what is happening here is happening everywhere."

Claims: C-1, C-3, C-4, C-5
Why: One city's one-year change and one neighborhood's feed are
carried to "this country" and to "ever." The central claim is
national and all-time, and it rests on that step. The local half
may be true and is checkable, but it cannot stand in for the
national series, and one year cannot stand in for the record.
Contrast: FBI, *UCR Summary of Reported Crimes in the Nation, 2025*
(released August 2026): murder and nonnegligent manslaughter fell
an estimated 18.1% nationwide from 2024 to 2025. In Table 4, murder
fell in every population group, from −9.2% (nonmetropolitan
counties) to −27.4% (cities of 500,000 to 999,999), and "No
population group designated within 'CIUS, 2025' showed an increase
in the year-to-year violent crime trend." These are group totals. A
single city can rise while its group falls, so they do not
contradict C-3; they contradict C-4.
<https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/LATEST/resources/reports/UCR_Summary_of_Reported_Crimes_in_the_Nation_2025.pdf>
Next step: the city police department's annual homicide counts for
the last ten or more years, and that agency's row in the FBI Crime
Data Explorer agency-level tables (offenses known to law
enforcement, by city). Look for whether 40 is high against the
city's own history, and keep the finding at the city boundary.

### 4 · Trade-off and substitution literacy — partial

> "The only answer is more police on every street, whatever it costs."

Claims: C-7 (side claim)
Why: The post names a remedy and waives its price in the same
sentence: no cost figure, and nothing about what would be cut or
raised to pay for it. The central claim is about how much crime
there is and stands or falls without the remedy, so the finding is
capped at partial. No position is taken here on the remedy.
Contrast: not checked in this run. The city is not named, so no
budget could be opened.
Next step: the city's adopted budget: the police department line,
sworn headcount, and cost per added officer, and which line or
revenue the increase would come from.

### 6 · Recency and updating literacy — partial

> "last year" · "in a single year" · "A few years ago"

Claims: C-2, C-3, C-5
Why: No figure carries a year and the post is undated, so a reader
cannot tell which edition any number belongs to. The one checkable
figure survives: "more than a million" still matches the latest FBI
release. The direction the post asserts does not reflect that
release, which reports falls, including in the two offenses the app
anecdote names.
Contrast: FBI, *UCR Summary of Reported Crimes in the Nation, 2025*
(released August 2026, data as of April 2, 2026): 1,119,768
estimated violent crimes in 2025; the violent crime rate fell 9.7%
from 2024, which the FBI calls the largest year-to-year decrease
since its estimates began in 1936; the 2025 murder rate of 4.1 per
100,000 "matches the rates observed in 1955 and 1956, making it
tied for lowest rates recorded since national estimates began"; the
2025 burglary rate (193.6) and motor vehicle theft rate (197.0) are
20-year lows. The release also revises the 2024 violent crime rate
(now 362.9) and flags its 2021 estimates as not comparable to other
years. Its preliminary January–June 2026 figures, against the same
months of 2025, show violent crime down 10.6% and murder down
23.0%. BJS's preliminary 2025 survey estimate (October 2026) is
25.7 violent victimizations per 1,000 persons age 12 or older, on a
redesigned instrument BJS says is not comparable to the earlier
series, with final figures due in 2027.
The same FBI figures show what a post written a few years earlier
would have been looking at: the murder rate reached a 20-year high
of 6.6 in 2020 and 2022, and the motor vehicle theft rate rose from
216.0 in 2019 to 321.9 in 2023 ((321.9 − 216.0) / 216.0 = 49%), the
highest since 2007.
<https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/LATEST/resources/reports/UCR_Summary_of_Reported_Crimes_in_the_Nation_2025.pdf> ·
<https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/preliminary-victimization-estimates-ncvs-2025>
Next step: put a year on each figure in the post. Then FBI Crime
Data Explorer, *Crime in the United States, 2025*, Table 1, and the
FBI's quarterly release for 2026; BJS, *Criminal Victimization,
2025*, when it is published.

### 7 · Source and evidentiary literacy — clear failure

> "My neighborhood app shows a new break-in or car theft every single day." · "the official numbers can't be trusted anyway"

Absent: a named source for any figure in the post.

Claims: C-2, C-3, C-5, C-6
Why: No figure is traced to where it was measured: the million has
no agency or report, and the city has no name. A feed and one
city's two-year comparison are placed above the systematic reports,
which C-6 sets aside as a class, although C-2 and C-3 are
themselves official counts.
Contrast: The systematic sources exist and are open to any reader.
The FBI's 2025 release draws on 17,075 law enforcement agencies
covering 96.2% of the population, with every city agency of
1,000,000 or more inhabitants reporting a full twelve months. BJS's
*Criminal Victimization, 2024* (September 2025) is the 52nd report
in a household survey series that began in 1973 and covers crimes
"reported and not reported to police."
<https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/LATEST/resources/reports/UCR_Summary_of_Reported_Crimes_in_the_Nation_2025.pdf> ·
<https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/criminal-victimization-2024>
Next step: name the report behind each figure. For C-2, FBI Crime
Data Explorer, *Crime in the United States, 2025*, Table 1. For
C-3, the city police department's published homicide counts. For
C-5, police incident data in place of the feed.

## Unverified

- "a new break-in or car theft every single day" and "A few years

- The cost of "more police on every street" — the city is not
  named — the city's adopted budget.
- The violent crime rate before 1991 — the FBI table opened starts
  in 1991 and the 2025 summary's figure starts in 2006; the FBI's
  "since 1936" statements opened here cover the murder rate and the
  size of the one-year decline, not the level of the violent crime
  rate — FBI Crime Data Explorer, historical estimates.
- The share of crime reported to police in 2025 — BJS has not yet
  published it; 2024 is the latest — BJS, *Criminal Victimization,
  2025*, due in 2027.

## A stronger version

A legitimate concern survives. A stronger claim would name the city
and the years, give murders per 100,000 residents across ten or
more years, and keep the conclusion at the city boundary. If it
makes a national claim, it would give the national rate beside
earlier years and name the report. It would use the victimization
survey for the unreported part instead of setting all official
figures aside, date every figure, and state what the proposed
remedy costs and what would pay for it.
