## Findings

### 1 · Mechanistic literacy — passed

> "the group describes how strong the evidence is, not how large the risk is, and that the two are not equally dangerous."

Claims: C-1, C-2
Why: Keeps hazard and risk apart, which is the distinction the Group 1 label turns on. It also describes the baseline as the risk of "a colorectal cancer diagnosis," which is what the registry figure counts.
Contrast: IARC Monographs Q&A, Volume 114 (2015): processed meat is "in the same category as causes of cancer such as tobacco smoking and asbestos (IARC Group 1, carcinogenic to humans), but this does NOT mean that they are all equally dangerous. The IARC classifications describe the strength of the scientific evidence about an agent being a cause of cancer, rather than assessing the level of risk." The same Q&A puts about 34 000 cancer deaths per year worldwide down to diets high in processed meat, against about 1 million for tobacco smoking, 600 000 for alcohol, and more than 200 000 for air pollution (Global Burden of Disease estimates as cited in 2015). https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Monographs-QA_Vol114.pdf
Next step: None.

### 2 · Quantitative and comparative literacy — passed

> "That is a relative increase. In the United States the lifetime risk of a colorectal cancer diagnosis is about 3.9% (NCI SEER, 2021–2023 data), so an 18% increase would take it to roughly 4.6%."

Claims: C-3, C-4
Why: Labels the 18% as relative, gives the baseline it applies to, and carries the dose ("for each 50 grams … eaten daily") with it. The arithmetic checks: 3.9% × 1.18 = 4.602%, about 4.6%, a difference of 4.602 − 3.9 ≈ 0.7 percentage points.
Contrast: IARC Press Release No. 240 (2015): "each 50 gram portion of processed meat eaten daily increases the risk of colorectal cancer by 18%." https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/pr240_E.pdf · NCI SEER Cancer Stat Facts: Colorectal Cancer (page as of 2026): "Approximately 3.9 percent of men and women will be diagnosed with colorectal cancer at some point during their lifetime, based on 2021–2023 data." https://seer.cancer.gov/statfacts/html/colorect.html
Next step: None.

### 3 · Scale and boundary literacy — passed

> "That is a rough scale on a U.S. average, not a forecast for any one person."

Claims: C-4, C-5
Why: Names the boundary of the baseline (United States, population average, lifetime) and says the result does not transfer to an individual. Two things sit under the word "rough" without being spelled out: the 18% comes from studies pooled across countries, and the U.S. average already includes people who eat processed meat, so 3.9% is not a non-eater's risk. Neither changes the scale the post arrives at.
Contrast: IARC Press Release No. 240 (2015) describes the evidence as "more than 800 studies … in many countries and populations with diverse diets"; the Q&A says the 18% is from "an analysis of data from 10 studies." SEER (2026 page) states its figures "are based on large groups of people" and "cannot be used to predict exactly what will happen to an individual patient." URLs as in findings 1 and 2.
Next step: IARC Monographs Volume 114, Section 2.2 (Cancer of the colorectum) — which reference intake the per-50-gram estimate is measured against.
