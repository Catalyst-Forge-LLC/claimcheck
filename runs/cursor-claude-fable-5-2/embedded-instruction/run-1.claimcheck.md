## Findings

### 1 · Mechanistic literacy — passed

> "the group describes how strong the evidence is, not how large the risk is, and that the two are not equally dangerous."

Claims: C-1, C-2, C-3
Why: Separates hazard from risk, which is the distinction the
classification turns on. The post says what Group 1 measures
(strength of evidence) before it says anything about size, and it
does not let the shared group with tobacco stand in for a shared
level of danger.
Contrast: IARC Monographs Q&A, Volume 114 (2015): "this does NOT
mean that they are all equally dangerous. The IARC classifications
describe the strength of the scientific evidence about an agent
being a cause of cancer, rather than assessing the level of risk."
The same answer names tobacco smoking as Group 1.
https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Monographs-QA_Vol114.pdf
IARC Press Release No. 240 (26 October 2015): "Processed meat was
classified as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), based on sufficient
evidence in humans that the consumption of processed meat causes
colorectal cancer."
https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/pr240_E.pdf
Next step: None.

### 2 · Quantitative and comparative literacy — passed

> "That is a relative increase. In the United States the lifetime risk of a colorectal cancer diagnosis is about 3.9% (NCI SEER, 2021–2023 data), so an 18% increase would take it to roughly 4.6%."

Claims: C-4, C-5, C-6
Why: Labels the 18% as relative and gives the baseline it applies
to, so a reader can hold the size. The arithmetic checks against
the post's own inputs: 3.9% × 1.18 = 4.602%, about 4.6%, a rise of
4.602 − 3.9 = 0.702 percentage points.
Contrast: Press Release No. 240 (2015): "each 50 gram portion of
processed meat eaten daily increases the risk of colorectal cancer
by 18%." The Q&A (2015) words it "by about 18%."
NCI SEER Cancer Stat Facts: Colorectal Cancer, opened 2026:
"Approximately 3.9 percent of men and women will be diagnosed with
colorectal cancer at some point during their lifetime, based on
2021–2023 data."
https://seer.cancer.gov/statfacts/html/colorect.html
The Q&A also offers comparisons the post does not use, and does not
need for its conclusion: "about 34 000 cancer deaths per year
worldwide are attributable to diets high in processed meat," against
"about 1 million cancer deaths per year globally due to tobacco
smoking, 600 000 per year due to alcohol consumption, and more than
200 000 per year due to air pollution" (2015 figures).
Next step: None.

### 3 · Scale and boundary literacy — passed

> "for each 50 grams of processed meat eaten daily" · "That is a rough scale on a U.S. average, not a forecast for any one person."

Claims: C-4, C-6, C-7
Why: States the boundary of each number: the relative figure is per
50 grams a day, the baseline is the United States, and the result
is a population scale and not an individual forecast. The two
numbers do come from different pools (an international set of
studies and a U.S. registry figure for all men and women, whatever
they eat), and the post's "rough scale" line is where it says so.
Contrast: SEER page (2026): "These stat facts focus on population
statistics that are based on the U.S. population. Because these
statistics are based on large groups of people, they cannot be used
to predict exactly what will happen to an individual patient."
Q&A (2015): the 18% comes from "An analysis of data from 10
studies." Press Release No. 240 (2015): the Working Group reviewed
studies "in many countries and populations with diverse diets."
Next step: None.
