# ClaimCheck — "STOP blaming your shower!!!"

**Source:** `post.md`, read 2026-11-11. Unchanged.
**Central claim:** > "So if you care about water that is actually used up, farms are the story, not your bathroom."
**Summary:** clear failures: 0 · partial: 1 · passed: 4 · N/A: 2
**Score:** 4.5 / 5 (2 N/A)

The one gap is recency: every figure is dated 2015 and matches the
report it cites, but USGS has since published 2020 estimates for the
same three categories and the post does not mention them. The 2020
figures leave the ordering the central claim rests on unchanged.
Mechanism, magnitude, boundary, and evidence pass; trade-off and
output do not apply, because the post ranks uses and proposes no cut.
This is a literacy review. It does not rule the claim true or false.

## Claims read

- **C-1** (central) > "So if you care about water that is actually used up, farms are the story, not your bathroom."
- **C-2** > "public supply — the water utilities deliver to homes and businesses — was 39.0 billion gallons a day of withdrawals."
- **C-3** > "Irrigation was 118 billion."
- **C-4** > "Thermoelectric power withdrew 133 billion a day, but most of that goes right back to the river: only 4.31 billion a day was actually consumed."
- **C-5** > "Irrigation consumed 73.2 billion a day."
- **C-6** > "Those are national totals for 2015; your own watershed may look very different, so check your local utility."

## Findings

### 1 · Mechanistic literacy — passed

> "Thermoelectric power withdrew 133 billion a day, but most of that goes right back to the river: only 4.31 billion a day was actually consumed."

Claims: C-1, C-4, C-5
Why: Keeps use and loss apart. It labels each figure as withdrawn or
consumed, and it draws the conclusion on "water that is actually used
up", which is the measure the consumed figures count.
Contrast: USGS Circular 1441 (2018, data for 2015): "Total consumptive
use for thermoelectric power was 4.31 Bgal/d in 2015 or 3 percent of
the total thermoelectric-power withdrawals." Arithmetic from the
post's own figures: 4.31 / 133 = 3.2%.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1441
Next step: None.

### 2 · Quantitative and comparative literacy — passed

> "public supply … was 39.0 billion gallons a day of withdrawals. Irrigation was 118 billion. Thermoelectric power withdrew 133 billion a day … only 4.31 billion a day was actually consumed. Irrigation consumed 73.2 billion a day."

Claims: C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5
Why: Each number sits beside a named larger and smaller case in the
same unit and year, and the set includes the largest withdrawer
(thermoelectric) instead of leaving it out. Public supply appears only
as withdrawals, but that favors the other side: if all 39.0 were
consumed, irrigation's consumed figure would still be larger
(73.2 / 39.0 = 1.9 times).
Contrast: Circular 1441 (2015 data): "Thermoelectric power,
irrigation, and public-supply withdrawals accounted for 90 percent of
total withdrawals in 2015", against a total of "about 322 billion
gallons per day". Check: (133 + 118 + 39.0) / 322 = 290 / 322 = 90%.
The abstract reports consumptive use for thermoelectric power and for
irrigation, and none for public supply. Other ratios from the post's
figures: 73.2 / 4.31 = 17 times; 73.2 / 118 = 62% of irrigation
withdrawals consumed. https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1441
Next step: None.

### 3 · Scale and boundary literacy — passed

> "Those are national totals for 2015; your own watershed may look very different, so check your local utility."

Claims: C-1, C-6
Why: States the boundary of its numbers (national, one year) and says
they do not settle a local case. "Your bathroom" is bounded by a
category that contains it: public supply covers "homes and
businesses", so the whole category is an upper limit, not a like
total.
Contrast: Circular 1441 (2015 data) lists homes on their own wells
separately: "Self-supplied domestic withdrawals were 3.26 Bgal/d."
Adding them: 39.0 + 3.26 = 42.26, still below 73.2. The same abstract
shows how uneven the national total is: "more than 50 percent of the
total withdrawals in the United States were accounted for by 12
States." https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1441
Next step: For a local reading, the USGS county-level data release for
2015 that accompanies Circular 1441, or the National Water
Availability Assessment Data Companion by county or hydrologic unit:
public supply against irrigation consumptive use for the reader's own
county.

### 6 · Recency and updating literacy — partial

> "In 2015, per the USGS national water-use report (Circular 1441)" · "Those are national totals for 2015"

Claims: C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5, C-6
Why: Dates every figure, twice. But 2015 is no longer the latest: USGS
now publishes 2020 estimates for public supply, irrigation, and
thermoelectric power, and the post does not note them. The newer
figures keep the same ordering, so the conclusion stands and this is
partial.
Contrast: USGS topic pages, 2020 figures, lower 48 states, modeled.
Irrigation: "about 110,904 million gallons per day (mgd) of water was
withdrawn for crop irrigation" and "Approximately 72% of the water
withdrawn for irrigation was consumptive use"; 0.72 × 110,904 =
79,851 mgd, about 79.9 billion gallons a day.
https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/irrigation-water-use
Public supply: "about 35,823 million gallons per day (mgd)" withdrawn,
"Consumptive use accounted for about 12% of the water withdrawn (4,241
mgd)".
https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/public-supply-water-use
Thermoelectric: "about 59,889 million gallons per day (mgd) of
freshwater was withdrawn" and "About 3.9% of the water withdrawn was
consumptive use (2,359 mgd)".
https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/thermoelectric-power-water-use
In 2020 terms irrigation's consumptive use is 79,851 / 35,823 = 2.2
times all public-supply withdrawals, 79,851 / 4,241 = 19 times
public-supply consumptive use, and 79,851 / 2,359 = 34 times
thermoelectric consumptive use. USGS cautions on each page: "2020
values are modeled estimates, so the change from 2015 partly reflects
different methods."
Next step: National Water Availability Assessment Data Companion,
calendar year 2020, national totals for the three categories: the
exact irrigation consumptive-use figure, which the topic page gives
only as a rounded share.

### 7 · Source and evidentiary literacy — passed

> "per the USGS national water-use report (Circular 1441)"

Claims: C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5

"Public-supply withdrawals in 2015 were 39.0 Bgal/d"; "Irrigation

thermoelectric power was 4.31 Bgal/d"; "Total consumptive use for
irrigation was 73.2 Bgal/d in 2015".
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1441
Next step: None.

## Unverified

  USGS topic pages that summarize them were opened, and the irrigation
  consumptive-use figure used here (79,851 mgd) is derived from a
  rounded "approximately 72%" — National Water Availability Assessment
  Data Companion, calendar year 2020.
- Whether the 2015 and 2020 totals cover the same area — the 2020
  pages say "lower 48 United States"; the Circular's coverage was not
  read from its abstract — Circular 1441, Introduction.
- The full Circular 1441 tables — the 42 MB report PDF was not opened;
  the five figures were checked against the abstract on the
  publication page — Circular 1441, Tables 1 and 2 and the category
  sections.

## A stronger version

The claim would pass recency by giving the 2020 USGS estimates next to
the 2015 Circular figures and saying that the two were produced by
different methods. Stating outright that the public-supply figure is
an upper limit on household use, withdrawn and not consumed, would
make the comparison in the central claim explicit instead of leaving
the reader to work it out.
