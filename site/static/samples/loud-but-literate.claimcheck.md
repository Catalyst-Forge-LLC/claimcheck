# ClaimCheck — "STOP blaming your shower!!!"

Worked sample, written to show where a literacy review stops and a
prose review starts. The source is an example written for
ClaimCheck: loud in register, sound in its numbers. Figures in
**Contrast** were checked on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11 against the
sources listed at the end.

**Source:** `loud-but-literate.md` (example), read 2026-11-11. Unchanged.
**Central claim:** > "So if you care about water that is actually used up, farms are the story, not your bathroom."
**Summary:** clear failures: 0 · partial: 2 · passed: 4 · N/A: 1
**Score:** 5 / 6 (1 N/A)

The claim holds on mechanism, magnitude, boundary, and evidence. It
ranks water uses without saying what the largest one produces, and
its 2015 figures now have a 2020 successor. Neither changes the
conclusion. The capital letters and exclamation marks are not
literacy findings and are not reported here. This is a literacy
review. It does not rule the claim true or false.

## Claims read

- **C-1** > "In 2015, per the USGS national water-use report (Circular 1441), public supply — the water utilities deliver to homes and businesses — was 39.0 billion gallons a day of withdrawals."
- **C-2** > "Thermoelectric power withdrew 133 billion a day, but most of that goes right back to the river: only 4.31 billion a day was actually consumed. Irrigation consumed 73.2 billion a day."
- **C-3** (central) > "So if you care about water that is actually used up, farms are the story, not your bathroom."
- **C-4** > "Those are national totals for 2015; your own watershed may look very different, so check your local utility."

## Findings

### 1 · Mechanistic literacy — passed

> "most of that goes right back to the river: only 4.31 billion a day was actually consumed"

Claims: C-2
Why: Separates water withdrawn and returned from water consumed,
and draws its conclusion from the consumed figures. That is the
distinction this dimension asks for.

Contrast: USGS Circular 1441 (2015): thermoelectric power withdrew
133 Bgal/d and consumed 4.31 Bgal/d; irrigation withdrew 118 Bgal/d
and consumed 73.2 Bgal/d.

Next step: None.

### 2 · Quantitative and comparative literacy — passed

> "public supply … was 39.0 billion gallons a day" · "Irrigation consumed 73.2 billion a day."

Claims: C-1, C-2, C-3
Why: Every figure has a unit, a year, and two other uses to compare
it with. The comparison is fair to the other side: even if all of
public supply's withdrawals were consumed, irrigation's consumption
would be larger (73.2 ÷ 39.0 ≈ 1.9).

Contrast: USGS Circular 1441 gives the three figures as quoted.

Next step: None.

### 3 · Scale and boundary literacy — passed

> "Those are national totals for 2015; your own watershed may look very different, so check your local utility."

Claims: C-4
Why: States the boundary of its numbers and says where they stop
applying. It does not carry a national total down to one place.

Contrast: not needed.

Next step: None.

### 4 · Trade-off and substitution literacy — N/A

Absent: any call for a cut or a remedy.

Claims: none
Why: The post says where consumed water goes. It asks nobody to
cut anything, so there is no substitute or cost to name.

Contrast: not needed.

Next step: None.

### 5 · Output and value accounting — partial

Absent: what irrigation produces, where the post says "farms are
the story".

Claims: C-3
Why: The post ranks uses by the water they consume and points at
the largest. It never says what that water delivers. It raises the
topic and leaves the benefit out. The conclusion, that most
consumed water goes to irrigation, stands either way, so this is
partial. One line would pass.

Contrast: USGS, "Irrigation Water Use": irrigation "is essential
for keeping fruits, vegetables, and grains growing to feed the
world's population."

Next step: add what irrigated farming produces, or say that the
post ranks volume only.

### 6 · Recency and updating literacy — partial

> "In 2015, per the USGS national water-use report (Circular 1441)"

Claims: C-1, C-2
Why: The figures are dated, and Circular 1441 is the last compiled
national report. USGS has since published modeled estimates for
2020 for the same three uses. They point the same way, so the
conclusion survives. An error that does not change the conclusion
is partial.

Contrast: USGS, 2020, lower 48 states: crop irrigation withdrew
110,904 Mgal/d, approximately 72% of it consumed (≈ 79,900 Mgal/d);
public supply withdrew 35,823 Mgal/d and consumed 4,241 Mgal/d;
thermoelectric power withdrew 59,889 Mgal/d of fresh water and
consumed 2,359 Mgal/d. USGS: "2020 values are modeled estimates, so
the change from 2015 partly reflects different methods." The 2020
figures cover the lower 48 states, and the 2015 figures the whole
country, so the two sets are not a like-for-like series.

Next step: USGS water-use pages for irrigation, public supply, and
thermoelectric power.

### 7 · Source and evidentiary literacy — passed

> "per the USGS national water-use report (Circular 1441)"

Claims: C-1
Why: Names the agency and the report, which a reader can open, and
the figures match it.

Contrast: USGS Circular 1441, *Estimated Use of Water in the United
States in 2015*.

Next step: None.
