title: About
description: Why ClaimCheck reviews literacy instead of ruling on truth, and why the source stays unchanged.

---

Most bad claims are not lies. They are missing a distinction: water borrowed versus water used up, a national total versus a local shortage, the input counted and the output ignored. A reader can feel that something is off and still not say what. ClaimCheck names it, one dimension at a time.

It does not rule a claim true or false. It classifies seven literacy questions, quotes the evidence, and names the primary source that would settle each one. When your agent cannot check a figure, the report says `unverified` instead of filling the gap.

A real concern can survive a failing claim. The worked example fails six of seven dimensions and still ends with the one question worth answering: what is the new facility doing to the local reservoir?

ClaimCheck sits on the shelf with [Smell Check](https://smellcheck.dev) (how a text is phrased), [TemperPass](https://temperpass.dev) (plans and assumptions), and the non-destructive reviewers [Cold-eye](https://coldeye.dev) and [Detangler](https://detangler.dev).
