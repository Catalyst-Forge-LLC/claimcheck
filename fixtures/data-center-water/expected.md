Injected: failures on five of seven dimensions, two partials.

Central claim: "wasting billions of gallons of water a year — water
that is gone forever". Side claim: the remedy, "stop using water
altogether".

Catch:

1 · mechanism — clear failure. "gone forever", "boiled off",
   "poured down the drain" treat use and loss as one thing:
   evaporative consumption is read as destruction, and withdrawal
   is not separated from consumption.
2 · magnitude — clear failure. "billions of gallons" with no
   denominator; no comparison with irrigation, thermoelectric
   power, public supply, or golf course irrigation.
3 · boundary — partial. The local reservoir is a legitimate,
   checkable local concern; "the whole country" flattens it into a
   national claim. Direct vs indirect (power-plant) water is not
   distinguished.
4 · tradeoff — partial. "stop using water altogether" calls for a
   cut and names no substitute: waterless cooling uses more
   electricity, which carries its own water at the power plant. It
   rests on a side claim, so it is capped at partial.
5 · output — clear failure. "Nothing useful comes out" and
   "homework or a bad poem" call the use waste because the product
   is intangible.
6 · recency — clear failure. Relies on an early, superseded
   per-conversation estimate with no date; ignores the 2024 LBNL
   national estimate and newer per-prompt disclosures.
7 · evidence — clear failure. "I saw it in a graphic" — secondary
   amplification; no primary source.

Score: 1 / 7.

The report should keep the local-reservoir concern alive and say
what a stronger claim would need (the facility's cooling design,
its permitted withdrawal and consumption, the watershed's stress
data). A report that only says "the claim is wrong" misses the
product.

A miss: a clear failure on trade-offs (the remedy is a side claim),
a single comparison under magnitude with no second one, or any
finding that takes a side on whether data centers should be built.
